# Getting Matched on TalEngineer — What Actually Determines Who Gets the Work

On most freelance platforms, getting work is a speed game: refresh the feed, bid fast, undercut on price. TalEngineer deliberately works differently. Matching here runs on verified signals — things the platform has checked, not things you typed — and the mechanics are public enough that you can plan around them. This article lays out those mechanics honestly: what gates you, what orders you, and what you control. It does not promise you a match rate or a timeline, because we don't invent numbers — and any platform that promises "get hired in X days" is telling you about its marketing, not its matching.

## Step zero: the AI screening at signup

Before you can be matched to anything, you pass the technical screener — a practical AI assessment in your claimed specialty, taken when you join. It presents realistic problems from your discipline and evaluates how you reason through them. Your screening score becomes part of your profile and feeds directly into your [TalScore](/playbook/how-talscore-is-computed).

The screener is the floor, not the ceiling: passing it makes you a member of the pool, not the first name on a shortlist. But take it seriously, because unlike everything else on this list, you sit it once — it is your permanent capability baseline on the platform.

## The hard gate: certification before assignment

This is the rule that shapes everything: **an engineer without a valid platform certification cannot be officially assigned to a project.** Not "ranked lower" — cannot be assigned. When an employer tries to assign an uncertified engineer, the platform refuses and points the engineer to the [Training Center](/training).

The practical consequence for you is blunt: **if you are not certified, you are not really in the market yet.** Applying to projects, being visible in searches, even being liked by an employer — none of it converts to a work order until you hold a valid platform certification (and one in the project's required track, when the employer specifies one). If you do only one thing after reading this article, sit the L1 exam in your strongest track. [How the exams work](/playbook/how-certification-exams-work) is documented in full.

## The ordering: TalScore and verified signals

Within the pool of eligible engineers, candidates are surfaced to employers ordered by verified quality signals — your AI screening score drives the default ordering, and your TalScore powers employer talent-pool shortlists, auto-invite eligibility, and the TalScore sort option. Employers also see your certifications by track and level, your screening result, your delivery history, and your reviews. Two things follow from this:

- **You climb by accumulating verifiable facts, not by gaming a feed.** A higher certification level moves your TalScore, and cleanly completed milestones and honest reviews move both your TalScore and your delivery record — real inputs in a published formula. There is no boost to buy and no algorithmic mood to chase.
- **The ordering compounds.** The engineers who get matched complete work, which improves the signals that get them matched again. The design intentionally rewards a track record — which means the best time to start building one is now, and the worst strategy is waiting for a "perfect" first project.

## What you control today: a complete, specific profile

Matching starts from the employer's side with a structured demand — the platform parses what they need into a scoped statement of work with concrete skills, a track, a region, and milestones. Your profile is matched against that structure. A profile that says "automation engineer, 10 years" gives the system almost nothing to match on; a profile that names your platforms (say, Siemens TIA Portal and Beckhoff TwinCAT), your industries, your region and travel radius, your languages, and your rate gives it everything.

Concretely: list every specific platform and toolchain you genuinely work with, set a rate that reflects your [regional market](/rates), keep your availability current, and write your project history in terms of what you delivered, not what you attended. Specificity is not decoration here — it is literally the surface the matching runs on.

## What "winning the work" means: milestone scoping

When a match becomes an engagement, the project arrives pre-structured: milestones with defined deliverables and amounts, each funded into escrow before its work begins. This changes what you're competing on. You are not being asked to quote a mysterious lump sum against unknown competitors — you are being assigned to scoped phases with the money already visible and locked. Read the milestone definitions carefully before accepting, flag scope ambiguity in the project chat *before* work starts, and treat the milestone structure as your friend: it is the thing that guarantees [you get paid](/playbook/how-engineer-payouts-work) for each accepted phase.

## The honest summary

The system reduces to four verbs, in order: **pass** the screener, **certify** in your track, **complete** your profile with real specifics, and **deliver** cleanly so your record compounds. There is no trick beyond that, and nothing here rewards being early to a feed or aggressive in a bidding war. TalEngineer is a curated marketplace for verified engineers — deliberately narrower than the mass platforms, so that when a match happens, both sides can trust it.

*Start with the gate that matters most: [get certified](/training). Then make sure your profile would match the projects you actually want.*
